**Operator:** NextLevel Media LA

## Lessons

### L-01 — Google Business Profile is the highest-leverage, zero-cost acquisition asset for local contractors

**Source:** Google Business Profile Help Center — "How Google determines local ranking"  
URL: https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091  
Date: page updated continuously; verified accessible Aug 2026

**What was learned / confirmed:**  
FACT — Google explicitly states three factors determine Local Pack ranking: relevance, distance, and prominence. Of these, relevance (how well a profile matches the search) and prominence (review count/rating, completeness) are operator-controllable at zero cost. Distance is not.

**Confidence:** HIGH — first-party Google documentation.

**Relevance to current workflow:**  
NextLevel Media LA's value proposition to contractors depends on GBP visibility. A client's profile that is incomplete or has few reviews will underperform regardless of how good the landing page or outreach is.

**Concrete improvement proposal:**  
Create a one-page GBP audit checklist (categories, service areas, hours, photos ≥ 5, responses to all reviews, Q&A seeded with common questions). Use this as a mandatory pre-work step before starting any outreach campaign for a contractor client.  
→ Suitable for a follow-up task/issue: "Add GBP audit checklist to client onboarding docs."

**Falsification / stop condition:**  
If a client with a fully optimised GBP profile receives zero Local Pack impressions for primary service queries after 90 days, investigate Google penalty or category mismatch — do not assume completeness alone is sufficient.

### L-02 — "Near me" queries deliver high purchase-intent traffic; service-area keywords must be explicit in GBP

**Source:** Google Search Central — "Optimize your site for local search"  
URL: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/local  
Date: verified accessible Aug 2026

**What was learned / confirmed:**  
FACT — Google Search Central advises that business profiles and landing pages should explicitly name the cities and neighbourhoods served. "Near me" queries are resolved by the user's location + the service-area fields in GBP, not by the domain name.

**Confidence:** HIGH — first-party Google Search Central documentation.

**Relevance:**  
NextLevel Media LA landing pages for electrician/EV-installer clients should name target neighbourhoods (e.g., Culver City, West Hollywood, Silver Lake) in page copy and meta tags, mirroring what is set in GBP service areas.

**Concrete improvement proposal:**  
Audit each active client landing page: confirm that at least 3–5 specific LA neighbourhoods or zip codes appear in the `<title>`, `<h1>`, and body copy. Cross-check that these neighbourhoods are also set as service areas in GBP.  
→ Suitable for a follow-up task/issue: "Add neighbourhood-level geo-copy audit to site QA process."

**Falsification / stop condition:**  
If a page with explicit neighbourhood copy ranks lower than a competitor page without it (holding all else equal), re-examine whether domain authority or review count is the dominant factor.

### L-03 — Contact forms: fewer required fields reduce drop-off (web.dev guidance)

**Source:** Google / web.dev — "Payment and address form best practices"  
URL: https://web.dev/payment-and-address-form-best-practices/  
Date: published 2020, last reviewed 2023

**What was learned / confirmed:**  
FACT (source guidance) — Google's own web.dev documentation explicitly recommends minimising required form fields and enabling `autocomplete` attributes to reduce friction and increase form completion rates.  
INFERENCE — Applying this to quote-request / contact forms on local contractor landing pages should reduce abandonment.

**Confidence:** HIGH (direction of effect per Google guidance), LOW (magnitude without site-specific data)

**Relevance:**  
The primary lead-capture mechanism for contractor clients is a contact or quote-request form. Any reduction in required fields is a reversible, zero-cost experiment.

**Concrete improvement proposal:**  
Reduce the required fields on the NextLevel Media LA quote-request form to: name + phone/email + service type. Add `autocomplete` attributes to all inputs. Optionally A/B test the shorter form if traffic permits (≥ 100 submissions/variant).  
→ Suitable for a follow-up code PR: "Reduce required fields and add autocomplete to contact form."

**Falsification / stop condition:**  
If form submissions do not increase after the change (measured over ≥ 200 total submissions post-change), revert and investigate whether other friction sources (page speed, trust signals, CTA copy) dominate.

### L-04 — Mobile page speed is a ranking factor and a conversion factor for local searches

**Source:** Google Search Central — "Page experience signals"  
URL: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience  
Date: updated 2023; Core Web Vitals still current as of 2026

**What was learned / confirmed:**  
FACT — Google includes Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS) as page experience ranking signals. For mobile-heavy local searches, a slow page loses both ranking and conversion.

**Confidence:** HIGH — first-party Google Search Central.

**Relevance:**  
Local contractor landing pages are predominantly accessed on mobile. A page that loads slowly on a phone will rank below competitors and cause visitors to bounce before seeing the CTA.

**Concrete improvement proposal:**  
Run a PageSpeed Insights audit (https://pagespeed.web.dev/) on the primary NextLevel Media LA contractor landing page. Target LCP < 2.5 s and CLS < 0.1 on mobile. Address the top 1–2 recommendations (typically: image compression, render-blocking scripts).  
→ Suitable for a follow-up task/issue: "Run PageSpeed audit and fix top 2 mobile performance issues."

**Falsification / stop condition:**  
If Core Web Vitals scores improve to "Good" range but organic mobile traffic does not increase within 60 days, re-evaluate whether the site's domain authority or content quality is the binding constraint.

### L-05 — Reviews are documented trust/visibility inputs; landing-page trust badges remain an inference to test

**Source:** Google Business Profile Help — "Get Google reviews"  
URL: https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050  
Also: Google Search Central — helpful-content guidance  
URL: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

**What was learned / confirmed:**  
FACT — Google's GBP documentation recommends actively requesting reviews and responding to them as a visibility and trust action.  
INFERENCE — Displaying verified review count, verified licence/insurance status, and a truthful response-time indicator on landing pages may improve trust; exact conversion lift is not established by the cited first-party sources.

**Confidence:** HIGH (review guidance — Google doc), MEDIUM/LOW (landing-page trust-badge conversion lift — inference pending experiment)

**Relevance:**  
High-ticket local services can benefit from clear, truthful trust evidence, but unsupported badges or invented response times would reduce credibility and violate evidence rules.

**Concrete improvement proposal:**  
Only display trust signals that are verified for the specific contractor: real review data, licence/insurance status that has been checked, and response-time claims backed by measured data. Add `LocalBusiness` JSON-LD only with factual fields. Treat any conversion effect as an experiment, not a guaranteed outcome.  
→ Suitable for a follow-up code PR only after the required factual inputs are available.

**Falsification / stop condition:**  
If verified trust signals do not improve call/form CTR over a defined test window with adequate traffic, keep the factual information but stop treating the presentation change as a conversion lever.
